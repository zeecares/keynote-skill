How to Speak

Patrick Winston's rules, as a deck that obeys them

Distilled from the MIT OpenCourseWare lecture

Open by promising: by the end you will know the handful of rules that decide whether your ideas get valued.

The promise

By the end, you will know the rules that get ideas valued and accepted.
Winston: start with an empowerment promise, not a joke. The audience is still adjusting to your voice in the first minute.

Cycle on the idea

  • Say it three times
  • 20% of the room is fogged out at any moment
Also: build a fence around your idea so it is not confused with somebody else's, and use verbal punctuation so people can get back on the bus.

Slides are condiments

  • We humans only have one language processor
  • Few words, big type, air
Winston's airport diagnosis of every bad deck: too many slides, too many words. Minimum font about 35-40pt; no background junk, no logos, no clutter, no reading the slide, no laser pointer - put an arrow on the image instead.

Winston's star: be remembered

  • Symbol
  • Slogan
  • Surprise
  • Salient idea - one that sticks out
  • Story
His own example: the arch work from his PhD thesis - symbol the arch, slogan "one-shot learning", surprise that one example is enough, salient idea the near miss, and the story of how it works.

The final slide stays up

  • Not "Thank you"
  • Not "Questions?"
  • It sits on screen through the whole Q&A
Collaborators go on the FIRST slide. End words: a joke works now; a salute to the audience works; "thank you for listening" implies they stayed out of politeness.
